# Cover letter — Methods in Ecology and Evolution

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Abstention-aware process identification recovers ecological information without false attribution in prospective known-truth tests,”** for publication in *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*.

Ecologists routinely interpret environmental predictors twice: as tools for prediction and as evidence about the processes shaping species distributions. Those interpretations can diverge when predictors are correlated, redundant, shared among process domains, or collectively inadequate. Our manuscript introduces an abstention-aware process-information framework that explicitly distinguishes five states — replaceable, contributory, required, unresolved and unavailable — and separates process identification from geographic transfer.

The paper's main contribution is not another variable-importance score. Conditional permutation importance and model class reliance already address important forms of dependence and across-model ambiguity; MaskSDM advances flexible feature-subset explanations. Our distinct target is a **declared ecological process and its full carrier closure**, with explicit adequacy, structural refusal and abstention states. We do not claim a head-to-head predictive advantage over those methods. The manuscript prospectively tests whether positive process information can be recovered without unsupported sharpening. Before opening the focal known-truth denominator, we froze the full method, eight world families, sample sizes, thresholds and six promotion gates. On unused seeds 74001–74020, the framework recovered 71/80 positive process targets while producing 0/700 favorable false positives, 0/60 over-resolutions of unresolved states, 0/60 structural-refusal violations and 0/120 sharp calls under unavailable systems. All six prospective gates passed.

We believe the manuscript fits *Methods in Ecology and Evolution* because it develops a broadly applicable inferential layer for ecological models rather than a taxon-specific workflow. The method is evaluated using prospective simulations specifically designed to separate unique information, redundancy, shared carriers, null correlation, interaction, observation confounding, missing drivers and geographic shift. The manuscript also draws a clear boundary around what the method does not do: it does not convert correlative occurrence models into mechanistic models or claim causal physiological recovery.

The manuscript's principal evidence is prospective known-truth validation. A later, separately frozen real-plant applicability test did not pass empirical promotion: although all 50 taxa passed environmental-feature completeness, process-first inference was available for only 10/50 taxa, and just 16/300 process cells had stable sharp states across the declared learners. We explicitly disclose this generalization limit in the Discussion rather than treating the real-data attempt as validation of our synthetic benchmark.
