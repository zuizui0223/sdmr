# Novelty and closest-literature positioning v1

## Central novelty

The paper should not be sold as a new way to rank environmental variables.

Its distinct object is **process-information identifiability under a declared predictor and observation system**.

The method asks whether process information is:

- replaceable;
- contributory;
- required;
- unresolved;
- unavailable.

The prospective result then evaluates positive recovery and false sharpening on separate denominators.

## Closest literature boundaries

### 1. Collinearity and correlated-predictor methods

Dormann et al. (2013) review methods for handling collinearity in ecological models.

**Overlap:** correlated predictors can destabilize variable-level interpretation.

**Difference:** removing correlation or selecting one predictor does not determine whether an ecological process remains represented redundantly, is shared across carriers, or is unavailable. SDMR's target is process-information state, not a preferred collinearity treatment.

### 2. Ecological model adequacy

Getz et al. (2018) argue that ecological models require explicit adequacy assessment and that model detail unsupported by data can be misleading.

**Overlap:** model adequacy constrains interpretation.

**Difference:** SDMR turns adequacy into an inferential state transition. If the declared full information system is inadequate, process interpretation becomes `unavailable`; the method is prospectively tested on an omitted-driver null.

### 3. Observational association versus biological mechanism

Blanchet, Cazelles & Gravel (2020) show that species co-occurrence is not itself evidence of ecological interaction. Related JSDM work likewise cautions that statistical associations need not be causal mechanisms.

**Overlap:** observational association should not be equated with biological process.

**Difference:** SDMR addresses environmental-process information rather than species interactions, and provides an operational five-state decision rule plus a prospective known-truth error-control test.

### 4. Mechanistic and process-explicit niche models

Mechanistic niche models use physiological, demographic or biophysical process information to parameterize species responses directly.

**Overlap:** both are concerned with processes rather than prediction alone.

**Difference:** SDMR does not claim to be a mechanistic niche model and does not infer causal physiology from occurrence data. It asks a narrower question: given an occurrence-only analysis and a declared mapping from environmental representations to process domains, what process information is identifiable?

## Wording to use

> SDMR does not turn correlative occurrence models into mechanistic models. It supplies an explicit inferential boundary for process-information claims made from a declared occurrence–environment representation.

> The contribution is not another collinearity filter. Redundancy can be scientifically meaningful because a process may remain represented even when one correlated predictor is removed.

> `Unavailable` is a property of the declared information system for the requested process inference, not evidence that all candidate processes are biologically absent.

## Wording to avoid

Do not claim:
- first method to distinguish prediction from explanation;
- first ecological use of abstention;
- first method to address correlated predictors;
- recovery of causal mechanisms from occurrence data;
- replacement for mechanistic niche models;
- universal validity outside the frozen known-truth design.
