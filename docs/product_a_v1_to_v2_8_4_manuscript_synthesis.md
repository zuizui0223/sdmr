## 1. Final manuscript thesis

Product A does not establish a universally superior SDM tuner. Its final positive result is more specific and more useful:

> **Environmental-process membership can be estimated by the ecological niche recovery lost when all declared representations of a process are removed from the prediction-adequate candidate class.**

The paper distinguishes four inferential objects:

1. predictive/functional adequacy;
2. exclusion-based process necessity;
3. process stability across ecological selectors;
4. counterfactual process membership under a declared candidate/process representation registry.

The fourth object is the final primary estimator. None of these is automatically a complete causal or fundamental-niche claim.

## 2. How the target changed prospectively

The original Product-A target was one full transferable procedure behind sealed spatial and unseen-taxon barriers. Known-truth development then falsified increasingly strong interpretations:

- v2.1–v2.2: prediction/environmental recovery and stable surfaces did not guarantee generating-process attribution;
- v2.3: ecological Pareto sharpening could narrow a certificate while losing truth coverage and creating false necessity;
- v2.4–v2.6: explicit exclusion controlled false-required necessity claims but could remain broad;
- v2.7.2: a consensus-first stable process core looked highly accurate under its original six-family generator suite, but temperature and water were true in all 60 cases and only soil varied in presence;
- stronger factorial truth test: temperature, water and soil were independently toggled over all seven non-empty process combinations, exposing that the predecessor stable intersection was not a sufficiently general process-membership estimator.

The final target therefore became a direct counterfactual question about the information contribution of each ecological process.

## 3. Evidence sequence

### Result 1 — Prediction/stability ≠ process truth

**Evidence:** v1, v2.1, v2.2.

Prediction metrics remain useful adequacy checks, but predictive transfer or stable response surfaces do not by themselves justify generating-process claims.

### Result 2 — Agreement among ecologically good models ≠ necessity

**Evidence:** v2.3.

Pareto certificates were sharper in all three controlled-truth panels but could lose truth/boundary coverage and create false necessary-process claims. Intersections within a performance-filtered set are conditional on that filtering rule.

### Result 3 — Exclusion-based necessity controls false-required claims but can remain broad

**Evidence:** v2.4–v2.6.

Necessity was reformulated as a process-information knockout question. Missing calibration remained `unavailable` rather than being converted into absence. After prospective calibration redundancy, v2.6 produced:

- complete process certificates 9/9;
- complete boundary certificates 9/9;
- false-required processes 0;
- possible-process recall 1.0;
- possible-process precision ≈0.467;
- `required_processes` empty in 9/9 validation taxa;
- wider calibrated intervals retained.

**Interpretation:** the exclusion branch controls false necessity under the declared known-truth contract, but it does not positively isolate a required driver in those panels.

### Result 4 — Consensus-first stability is useful but not sufficient as a general classifier

**Evidence:** v2.7.1–v2.7.2 plus the later factorial stress test.

v2.7.1 established that process-dependent numerical behavior could alter a discrete selected-predictor result. v2.7.2 then prospectively fixed estimator/RNG identity and produced exact computational parity.

The v2.7.2 stable process core exactly matched the hidden process set in 55/60 cases, with pooled P=0.9889 and R/F1=0.9833. However, temperature and water were invariant true processes across all 60 cases, so this suite did not independently test presence/absence classification for all major process identities.

A stronger factorial generator then varied `temperature`, `water` and `soil` over all seven non-empty combinations. On seeds 4201–4205 (`n=35`):

- predecessor stable intersection exact: **22/35 = 62.9%**;
- AUC-selected winner exact: **25/35 = 71.4%**;
- predecessor process specificity/sensitivity weaknesses became explicit.

**Interpretation:** the earlier 55/60 result remains a valid proof of concept and observation-confounding result, but it is not the final general process-identification evidence.

### Result 5 — Counterfactual niche-recovery loss directly identifies process membership

For a declared process `p`, Product A now:

1. admits only candidates passing the existing independent prediction-adequacy gate;
2. separates adequate candidates into those carrying `p` and those excluding every declared representation of `p`;
3. within each of five frozen sampling/background perturbations, compares the best held-out Schoener-D niche overlap achievable with versus without `p`;
4. normalizes that overlap gap by the overlap range among all adequate candidates;
5. averages across perturbations;
6. classifies process membership using process-specific thresholds frozen from discovery seeds 4201–4205 only.

The declared alias registry means temperature exclusion also excludes `temp_proxy`.

Frozen thresholds:

- temperature `0.26539643681319824`;
- water `0.06716709986237807`;
- soil `0.33424158409183774`.

This score is truth-free at application time; hidden truth is used only to evaluate the completed process set.

## 4. Final integrated conceptual model

### Prediction adequacy

Does the fitted candidate adequately transfer withheld observations?

### Exclusion-based necessity

Does an adequate ecological explanation survive after all declared information for process `p` is removed?

This can produce `required`, `refuted as necessary`, `possible` or `unresolved` states and is deliberately conservative.

### Consensus-first process stability

Do independent ecological selection principles support the same process information?

This is useful diagnostic/stability evidence but is not the final classifier and does not prove necessity.

### Counterfactual process membership

How much ecological niche recovery is lost when `p` is unavailable through every declared representation in the adequate candidate class?

This is the final process-identification estimator.

### Representation boundary

Raster, proxy, derived exposure, composite and ecological process are different objects. Current exclusion is relative to a declared registry. Complete real-world proxy closure remains future work.
