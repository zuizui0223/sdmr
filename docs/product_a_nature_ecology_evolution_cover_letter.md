# Draft cover letter — Nature Ecology & Evolution

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, **“Predictive success does not identify ecological necessity in species distribution models,”** for publication in *Nature Ecology & Evolution*.

Species distribution modelling has long distinguished ecological explanation from prediction, and simulation studies have shown that discrimination accuracy can be largely uninformative about the functional accuracy of inferred environmental responses. Our study addresses the next inferential step: **even when models are selected for ecological recovery rather than prediction, when is an environmental process actually identifiable as necessary?**

Using prospectively sealed occurrence evidence and controlled-truth generators, we first confirm that predictive transfer and stable environmental response surfaces can coexist with incorrect process attribution. We then show a stronger and less intuitive failure: restricting inference to models with better ecological-recovery scores can itself create false necessary-process claims. This failure motivates our central contribution. We define process-information necessity by falsification against adequate alternatives: a process is not necessary because it appears in a winning or consistently good model, but only insofar as adequate ecological explanations become unavailable when the declared information carried by that process is excluded. Insufficient evidence remains unresolved rather than being converted into absence.

The exclusion-based necessity certificate and the later process-stability certificate address different questions and are not treated as the same estimator. With prospective calibration, falsification-first exclusion eliminated false-required processes while retaining complete true-process recall, although the admissible process set remained broad. Separately, a consensus-first certificate asked whether process information could remain stable across canonical and perturbation-robust ecological selectors without requiring exact fitted-model agreement. Across 60 unused controlled-truth cases from six niche-generating families, that stable process core had precision 0.9889 and recall 0.9833; four of six families had mean precision and recall of 1.0. Exact fitted-model consensus occurred in only 38/60 cases, whereas process-set consensus occurred in 50/60. Independent computational processes reproduced all compared floating and discrete scientific outputs exactly. This result supports process-information stability, not a claim that the exclusion-based necessity estimator itself achieved 0.9889 precision.

We then subjected the ecological selection architecture to a prospectively frozen fresh empirical endpoint spanning 12 plant taxa, three spatial split seeds and three accessible-area assumptions. The preregistered test did not support a strict ecological advantage over the AUC-selected comparator, and Product A was not promoted. We retain this negative result without rescue. A reporting-only audit of the frozen full-denominator artifacts explains the result: ecological and AUC selection chose exactly the same candidate and predictor set in all 108 matched taxon × accessible-area × seed cells. Thus the empirical dataset produced observational equivalence between distinct selection objectives rather than a realized comparison between different models.

We believe this has broad relevance beyond a particular SDM algorithm. Ecological analyses routinely interpret selected variables from correlated measurements, proxies and composite representations. Existing model-class and variable-importance frameworks appropriately acknowledge that many predictive models may fit well, but they do not turn process necessity into a falsifiable ecological claim. Our results show prospectively why prediction, functional recovery, model agreement, process stability and process necessity must remain separate inferential objects. Together, the exclusion and consensus branches provide a concrete route for reporting which process claims are refuted, which remain possible or unresolved, and which process information is stable across defensible ecological analyses.

The manuscript does not claim a complete causal mechanism, a fundamental niche, or universal empirical superiority. Necessity is explicitly scoped to a declared representation/evidence system, and the absence of literal empirical process truth is treated as a boundary rather than hidden. The scientific record is protected by frozen contracts, unused known-truth validation seeds, immutable workflow artifacts and a final empirical endpoint that was not rerun or retuned after non-support.

All code, contracts and evidence provenance are retained in the public repository, and the exact submission state and source-data tables will be archived with a permanent DOI before submission. The paper is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for considering our work.
