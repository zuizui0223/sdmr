# Draft cover letter — Nature Ecology & Evolution

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, **“Predictive success does not identify ecological necessity in species distribution models,”** for publication in *Nature Ecology & Evolution*.

Ecologists routinely use predictive models not only to forecast occurrence but also to infer the environmental processes that structure species distributions. Our study shows prospectively that these are different inferential tasks. Using sealed occurrence evidence and controlled-truth generators, we demonstrate that predictive transfer and stable response surfaces can coexist with incorrect process attribution, and that restricting inference to ecologically better-performing models can create false claims of environmental necessity. We therefore reformulate niche interpretation as a falsification problem: a process is not necessary because it appears in the winning model, but only insofar as adequate alternative explanations fail when the declared information carried by that process is excluded.

The resulting falsification-first, set-valued framework was evaluated across 60 unused controlled-truth cases from six niche families. Stable process cores were recovered with precision 0.9889 and recall 0.9833; four of six families had mean precision and recall of 1.0. Importantly, exact fitted-model consensus occurred in only 38/60 cases, whereas process-set consensus occurred in 50/60, showing that ecological information can remain identifiable without a unique model. Independent computational processes reproduced all compared floating and discrete outputs exactly.

We then subjected the framework to a prospectively frozen fresh empirical endpoint spanning 12 plant taxa, three spatial split seeds and three accessible-area assumptions. The preregistered test did not support a strict ecological advantage over the AUC-selected comparator, and Product A was not promoted. Inspection of the frozen full-denominator evidence showed why: ecological and AUC selection chose exactly the same candidate and selected-predictor set in all 108 matched taxon × accessible-area × seed cells. This empirical collapse is not evidence that AUC identifies ecological truth; rather, it demonstrates a complementary identification limit in which distinct selection objectives are observationally indistinguishable.

We believe the study has broad relevance because the problem is not specific to one SDM algorithm. Ecological analyses commonly interpret selected variables from correlated measurements, proxies and composite representations. Whenever multiple representations can support the same observations, predictive model selection and ecological identification are distinct problems. Our results provide a prospective empirical and controlled-truth demonstration of why ecological necessity should be challenged against adequate alternatives and why unresolved or substitutable states must be retained rather than forced into a single winning explanation.

The manuscript reports the frozen negative empirical endpoint without rerunning, retuning or replacing the consumed evidence. All scientific transitions were prospectively protected by explicit information barriers, and code and evidence provenance are retained in the public repository. The paper is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for considering our work.

Sincerely,
