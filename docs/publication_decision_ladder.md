# Product A publication ladder

## Route A — highest-challenge ecology route

A one-shot editorial challenge to **Nature Ecology & Evolution** or **Ecology Letters** is justified only if the fresh empirical result changes a broadly used ecological modelling principle, rather than merely showing that one workflow performs well.

### Trigger

All of the following should be substantially true:

- a complete fresh empirical full-denominator Product-A confirmation reaches a valid supported terminal decision;
- the supported result reproduces the known-truth direction rather than depending on a narrow diagnostic subset;
- a conventional practice such as correlation/VIF-first exclusion or a restricted predictor universe is shown to discard transferable ecological information in a consistent and practically meaningful way;
- the conclusion survives the frozen M sensitivity and prediction/ecological guardrails;
- the result is broad enough across the predeclared fresh plant panel to support a general ecological-modelling statement;
- the paper can answer a field-level question such as whether ecologists should exclude correlated environmental variables before testing their independent predictive information;
- no Product-B biological universality claim is imported prematurely.

### Intended claim level

A suitable high-level claim would be of the form:

> Environmental variables should be admitted or excluded by prospectively independent predictive/ecological information rather than by correlation structure alone.

The strongest route requires evidence that this changes ecological inference in a general way, not only software performance.

### Submission sequence

1. Nature Ecology & Evolution **or** Ecology Letters, according to whether the final emphasis is a broad ecological modelling principle or a concise conceptual advance;
2. if rejected mainly on breadth/selectivity, retarget promptly to Methods in Ecology and Evolution without collecting an extra favorable cohort solely for prestige.

## Route B — Methods in Ecology and Evolution

**Methods in Ecology and Evolution is the default strong Product-A route** once a valid fresh empirical terminal decision exists.

This route is appropriate when Product A demonstrates a rigorous new methodological framework even if the empirical advantage is moderate or conditional.

Core contribution:

- explicit model-pool/sealed-answer information barrier;
- candidate-universe comparison rather than silent prefiltering;
- nested predictive selection and conventional baselines;
- structural transportability separated from ecological confirmation;
- full-denominator and fail-closed unavailable semantics;
- exact method/predictor-universe inheritance for downstream analysis;
- known-truth plus fresh empirical validation.

A complete null or mixed empirical result can still be methodologically valuable if it sharply bounds when the proposed procedure does or does not improve independent ecological recovery. Do not hide a full-denominator null in favor of development subsets.

## Route C — Ecological Informatics / Ecography

Use **Ecological Informatics** if the final Product-A contribution is strongest as a computational SDM benchmarking, information-barrier, provenance and uncertainty framework.

Use **Ecography** if the strongest result concerns transferable spatial/environmental information across plant distributions and has a clear biogeographic interpretation, but does not reach the generality threshold for a broad-ecology journal.

## Technical STOP rule

A runtime timeout, cancellation, unavailable source, or other presealed technical STOP:

- is not a scientific negative;
- does not lower the Product-A target journal by itself;
- does not permit threshold/taxon/M/seed retuning;
- may justify a prospectively frozen runtime-only successor preserving the same science;
- should be reported as execution provenance if relevant, but the methods paper should not substitute a technical STOP for the required empirical terminal result.

## Product-A development stop rule

Once Product A has:

1. a valid fresh empirical terminal decision under a prospectively frozen full-denominator design; and
2. a separate explicit Product-A promotion/non-promotion decision,

stop expanding Product-A methodology unless the terminal result exposes a specific falsified assumption that must be resolved scientifically.

If supported, prepare the Product-A manuscript immediately. If not promoted, retain the complete non-support result and close the manuscript through Route B or C without further dataset hunting. Product B can proceed only after explicit unblock.

# Product B publication ladder

## Entry condition

No Product-B journal route exists until Product B is scientifically unblocked. Known-truth support, structural feasibility, partial empirical subsets, or a Product-A technical recovery do not satisfy this condition.

## Route A — highest-challenge ecological synthesis

Attempt **Nature Ecology & Evolution** or **Ecology Letters** if Product B yields a broad, conceptually important result about environmental information across plant distributions.

Examples of qualifying outcomes include:

- a small environmental process core that reproducibly transfers to diverse unseen plant taxa after predictor substitution is respected;
- strong evidence that no single universal process core exists, with the apparent universality decomposing into reproducible biome, life-form, taxonomic or other predeclared conditional structure;
- a result that changes how ecologists interpret apparent universal drivers from correlated raster families.

A "no universal core" result is not inherently negative. It can be the strongest result if the design makes the absence of universality identifiable and reveals a reproducible alternative structure.

The top route requires broad taxonomic/ecological scope and a general ecological conclusion, not a list of frequently selected rasters.

## Route B — Global Ecology and Biogeography

**Global Ecology and Biogeography is the default strong Product-B route** for broad-scale, cross-taxon conclusions about which environmental information generalizes and why.

This is especially suitable if the main result is:

- global versus conditional process cores;
- broad taxonomic transfer;
- predictor-substitution effects on apparent driver generality;
- general macroecological conclusions that remain relevant beyond the sampled taxa/regions.

## Route C — Ecography

Use **Ecography** if Product B yields a strong but more spatially/process-conditional biogeographic result, or if the methodological/spatial interpretation is more central than a global macroecological synthesis.

## Product-B stop rule

Do not chase a universal core by changing process definitions, predictor groups, taxa, thresholds or strata after unseen-taxon outcomes are opened.

The valid Product-B outcomes include:

- compact global core;
- global + conditional cores;
- only conditional cores;
- no useful universal core.

The manuscript and journal route follow whichever of these is actually supported.
