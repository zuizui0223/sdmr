# Counterfactual niche recovery identifies environmental processes beyond model selection

Status: **Nature Ecology & Evolution Article draft / prospective controlled-truth successor integrated / empirical endpoint unchanged**

## Abstract

Species distribution models are often selected for prediction and then interpreted as evidence about environmental processes. We developed a sealed estimator that asks how much attainable held-out niche recovery is lost when every declared representation of a candidate process is excluded, after enforcing prediction adequacy. A factorial truth test first falsified our preceding model-intersection approach: with temperature, water and soil varied independently across all seven non-empty combinations, it recovered only 22/35 complete process sets. Counterfactual thresholds were calibrated on these discovery cases and frozen before new truth was opened. On 35 fresh cases, the estimator recovered 30/35 complete generating-process sets. Without changing the method or thresholds, an independent 70-case replication recovered 65/70 (92.9%), versus 56/70 (80.0%) for AUC-selected winners and 49/70 (70.0%) for the predecessor. Replication sensitivity/specificity were 1.000/0.933 for temperature, 1.000/0.933 for water and 0.975/1.000 for soil; process truth remained exact in 27/30 cases despite ecological-model disagreement. Fresh plant data still yielded identical ecological and AUC-selected models in 108/108 matched cells. Counterfactual ecological recovery can identify process membership under controlled truth without a unique winning model.

Ecological models are often asked to predict observations and explain the environmental structure behind them. Species distribution models (SDMs) make this distinction especially consequential because environmental constraints are routinely interpreted from occurrence data even when direct experiments are unavailable. Explanation and prediction have long been recognized as distinct roles of SDMs, and discrimination accuracy can be weakly informative about the functional accuracy of environmental responses [1,2]. Spatial partitioning, model tuning and reproducible evaluation have consequently become increasingly rigorous [3]. Yet a more specific identification problem remains: when several adequate models use correlated variables, proxies or composites, which underlying environmental processes are actually required to recover the occupied niche?

Selected-variable importance does not answer that question. Many well-performing models can support different variable-importance conclusions, while spatial autocorrelation, collinearity and positional uncertainty can further alter apparent species–environment relationships [4–7]. Nor does replacing a predictive winner with an ecologically selected winner automatically solve the problem, because model selection still conditions inference on a particular retained model class. What is needed is an estimand attached directly to the environmental process rather than to the winning model.

We developed such an estimand through prospective falsification. Whole spatial blocks were sealed before model construction; candidate selection could not use hidden generating truth; accessible-area and sampling assumptions were treated as perturbations rather than optimized after outcome; and failure was an admissible result. Early controlled-truth stages showed that predictive transfer and stable response surfaces could coexist with wrong process attribution, and that restricting inference to ecologically better models could create false necessary-process claims (Figs. 1, 2). A later process-intersection certificate performed well in one six-family truth suite, but temperature and water were true in every case. We therefore challenged the method with a stronger factorial design in which temperature, water and soil independently switched on and off. That test failed—and motivated the counterfactual estimator evaluated here.

## Results

### Prediction and model agreement failed as process identifiers

The first controlled-truth stages separated predictive adequacy from process attribution. Candidates could transfer to withheld occurrences or produce stable environmental response surfaces while assigning the pattern to the wrong generating process. Prediction therefore remained an adequacy requirement, not evidence of process identity.

Replacing one winner with a set of ecologically adequate models did not remove this problem. Ecological Pareto filtering narrowed between-model response ranges in all tested panels but could remove viable alternatives, lose generating-process or response-boundary coverage, and create false necessary-process claims (Fig. 2). Agreement within a performance-filtered set was therefore conditional on the filtering operation rather than proof of ecological necessity.

A falsification-first exclusion certificate solved part of this problem by protecting against false necessity. After prospective calibration, it produced zero false-required processes and complete possible-process recall, but the possible set remained broad (precision approximately 0.467) and `required_processes` was empty in all nine validation taxa. Thus process necessity remained a conservative, set-valued estimand rather than a positive driver-discovery result.

A separate consensus-first certificate then asked whether process information could be more stable than exact model identity. In a six-family unused-truth suite it recovered complete generating-process sets in 55/60 cases, versus 50/60 for AUC-selected fitted candidates, and remained exact in 19/22 cases where two ecological selectors chose different fitted models. However, temperature and water were true in all 60 cases. The apparently strong pooled result therefore did not establish general process presence-versus-absence identification.

### Factorial truth falsified the stable-process intersection

We prospectively constructed the complete non-empty factorial universe of three environmental processes: `{temperature}`, `{water}`, `{soil}`, `{temperature,water}`, `{temperature,soil}`, `{water,soil}` and `{temperature,water,soil}`. Each process therefore varied independently between present and absent truth states. A fixed candidate library represented individual processes, process combinations, a correlated temperature proxy and irrelevant broad predictors; five predeclared perturbations varied sampling bias and accessible/background extent. Hidden process truth was unavailable to every selector.

The preceding stable-process intersection failed this stronger test. Across 35 discovery cases, it recovered the complete process set in only **22/35 (62.9%)**, compared with **25/35 (71.4%)** for the AUC-selected fitted candidate. When the canonical and perturbation-robust ecological selectors chose different fitted models, the stable intersection remained exactly correct in only **12/20 (60.0%)** cases. Temperature sensitivity was 1.00 but specificity only 0.667; water sensitivity was 0.70; soil specificity was 0.867.

This was a substantive falsification rather than a tuning inconvenience. The earlier 55/60 result had demonstrated that shared process information could survive model ambiguity when major process truths were largely invariant. It had not solved the harder problem of deciding whether each process was present or absent. We therefore retained the 22/35 non-support and changed the estimand rather than relaxing thresholds or selecting favorable process combinations.

### Counterfactual ecological recovery identified process membership

For each declared process, we asked how much ecological recovery becomes unattainable if every representation of that process is prohibited. Within each sampling/background perturbation, candidate models first had to pass the existing prediction-adequacy gate. We then compared the best held-out Schoener-D environmental-niche overlap achievable by adequate process-containing candidates with the best overlap achievable by adequate candidates excluding the process. Predictor aliases were collapsed prospectively, so excluding temperature also excluded its declared temperature proxy. The difference was normalized by the overlap range among all adequate candidates and averaged across the five perturbations (Fig. 3a).

This counterfactual score contains no hidden process truth. The 35 factorial discovery cases were used only to calibrate one threshold per process. Discovery scores showed complete separation between true and false process states: temperature false maximum 0.2522 versus true minimum 0.2786; water 0.0525 versus 0.0818; soil 0.2068 versus 0.4617. Midpoints were therefore frozen at 0.265396 for temperature, 0.067167 for water and 0.334242 for soil before any new truth was opened.

Fresh validation used five unused seeds across all seven process sets, yielding another 35 cases. The predeclared gate required complete denominator, complete process-set recovery of at least 0.80, and sensitivity and specificity of at least 0.80 for every process. **Every gate passed.** The counterfactual estimator recovered the complete generating-process set in **30/35 (85.7%)**, compared with **23/35 (65.7%)** for the predecessor stable core and **25/35 (71.4%)** for AUC-selected winners. Temperature sensitivity/specificity were 1.000/0.933, water 1.000/0.800 and soil 1.000/0.933. Ecological fitted models disagreed in 18 cases, but process truth remained exact in **15/18 (83.3%)**.

The improvement was not obtained by reopening the validation outcome. Thresholds, process sets, candidate library and perturbations were locked after the discovery phase. The estimator was next taken unchanged to a second independent replication.

### Independent replication recovered 65 of 70 complete process sets

Ten new seeds were prospectively frozen across the same seven process combinations, producing **70 replication cases**. The method and all process thresholds were unchanged from the 35-case validation. Every replication support gate again passed (Fig. 3b,c).

The counterfactual estimator recovered **65/70 (92.9%)** complete generating-process sets. The AUC-selected winner recovered **56/70 (80.0%)**, and the predecessor stable-process intersection **49/70 (70.0%)**. Canonical and perturbation-robust ecological selectors disagreed on fitted-model identity in 30 cases, yet the counterfactual process set remained exactly correct in **27/30 (90.0%)**.

Process-specific classification was similarly strong. Temperature was truly present in 40 cases and absent in 30: all 40 true cases were identified, with 28/30 true negatives (sensitivity 1.000, specificity 0.933). Water had the same 40/40 true-positive and 28/30 true-negative counts. Soil was identified in 39/40 true cases with no false positives among 30 absent cases (sensitivity 0.975, specificity 1.000). Thus all three independently varying processes exceeded both sensitivity and specificity gates, rather than relying on invariant truths.

The paired outcome against AUC further localized the difference. Both procedures recovered the complete process set in 51 cases; the counterfactual estimator alone was exact in 14, AUC alone in 5, and there were no cases in which both were wrong. This paired comparison is descriptive rather than a newly declared universal-superiority test. The predeclared result is the absolute counterfactual recovery and process-specific operating characteristics.

Performance was also interpretable across process combinations. Exact recovery was 8/10 for temperature alone, 8/10 for water alone, 10/10 for soil alone, 10/10 for each two-process combination, and 9/10 when all three processes generated the niche. The five errors were fully localized: two temperature-only cases gained a false water call, two water-only cases gained a false temperature call, and one three-process case missed soil. The estimator therefore failed primarily by adding the correlated climate partner in single-process niches rather than by broadly losing true processes.

The resulting method does not say that a process is causal because it appears in a model. It says that, under the frozen candidate and representation registry, **adequate ecological recovery degrades enough when the process is made unavailable to classify that process as part of the generating set**. This is process membership; the stronger claim of biological necessity remains separately protected by the exclusion certificate.

### Fresh occurrence data remained empirically non-identifying

Controlled truth provides a literal process answer key. Fresh empirical plant occurrence data do not. We therefore retained the previously frozen empirical endpoint as an external-validity boundary rather than using the new simulation result to rewrite it.

The empirical endpoint contained 12 plant taxa, three spatial split seeds and three 150/300/500-km accessible-area assumptions. Prediction adequacy passed and ecological selection was nondominated relative to AUC in all three primary parts, but strict ecological improvement occurred in **0/3**. The terminal scientific decision remains `empirical_confirmation_not_supported`, with separate Product-A decision `not_promoted`.

Full-denominator artifact audit explains this non-support. Ecological and AUC roles selected exactly the same candidate and predictor set in **108/108** matched taxon × accessible-area × seed cells, and all audited sealed metrics were identical between roles (Fig. 4). Thus the empirical corpus supplied neither literal process truth nor a realized model contrast with which to evaluate the counterfactual controlled-truth advantage. The new process-identification result does not convert this endpoint into empirical confirmation.

## Discussion

Product A now provides a quantitative answer to the problem that motivated its development. Environmental-process inference need not be attached to the identity of one selected SDM. Instead, a process can be tested by asking what ecological recovery remains possible when all declared representations carrying that process are removed. Under independently varying temperature, water and soil truth, this counterfactual ecological-recovery loss recovered 85.7% of complete process sets in fresh validation and 92.9% in an unchanged 70-case replication.

The prospective failure sequence is important to the interpretation. Prediction and stable response surfaces first proved insufficient for process attribution. Ecological model-set sharpening then created false necessity. A consensus-first process intersection appeared strong in a six-family test but fell to 62.9% exact recovery when all three process identities were allowed to switch independently. The counterfactual estimator was developed only after that falsification, frozen on the discovery cases, validated on unused truth, and then replicated unchanged on a second set of unused seeds. The final evidence therefore concerns recovery of process membership, not retrospective relabelling of a favorable model-selection result.

The counterfactual formulation also clarifies what the method is measuring. The target is not variable importance and not a weighted score combining prediction with ecology. Prediction is a gate. Among models that clear that gate, the statistic measures loss of best attainable held-out ecological niche overlap under process exclusion. Correlated variables can be grouped prospectively into a common process representation; in the present test, the temperature proxy was excluded whenever the temperature process was challenged. This moves the inferential object from a selected coefficient or model to a process-level loss of explanatory ecological capacity.

The method is not infallible. In replication, correlated temperature and water were each falsely added twice to single-process niches, and soil was missed once when all three processes were true. These five failures define a concrete current boundary. More importantly, the declared process registry is not a complete representation of every real ecological proxy or composite. A real-data process claim would require a prospectively declared registry sufficiently rich to make process exclusion scientifically meaningful.

Process membership also remains distinct from process necessity. The earlier exclusion certificate asked whether any adequate alternative survives when process information is removed and correctly refused to assert necessity when the evidence remained broad. The new counterfactual statistic instead quantifies how strongly ecological recovery degrades under that removal and classifies membership from thresholds calibrated on independent discovery truth. A process can therefore be strongly supported as part of the generating set without being claimed as physiologically indispensable in nature.

The empirical result preserves the final limitation. The fresh plant endpoint did not produce different ecological and AUC-selected models, and real occurrence data contain no hidden process labels. The controlled-truth success therefore establishes what the estimator can recover when the answer exists; it does not establish that any particular real plant distribution has been causally explained. Empirical validation will require systems in which process truth is independently constrained by experiments, interventions, natural experiments or other external evidence rather than by the SDM itself.

The practical conclusion is nevertheless positive and testable. For occurrence-based ecological models with multiple adequate representations, **process identification can be framed as a counterfactual recovery problem**: retain prediction adequacy, remove the complete declared information channel for one process, and measure the ecological recovery that becomes unattainable. In the present prospective tests, that shift raised exact process-set recovery from 62.9% for the falsified predecessor to 85.7% in fresh validation and 92.9% in unchanged independent replication, while retaining explicit empirical non-identification where nature supplied no answer key.
