## Secondary diagnostic: supplying known sampling effort

A separate frozen sensitivity supplied the simulator's true cell-specific sampling effort as an additional nuisance covariate, retained in every full and knockout fit. This changes the representation universe and is **not** a like-for-like accuracy comparison against the unchanged ODO reference.

It did not eliminate sharp disagreement with the ODO-unresolved observation-confounded thermal target.

Random-cell:
- all three observation-confounded seeds became **replaceable** for thermal under both learner pairs;
- relative to the unchanged ODO unresolved reference, this is 3/3 sharp overresolution.

Spatial:
- seed 92002 became **replaceable**;
- seeds 92001 and 92003 remained unresolved;
- 1/3 overresolution under both pairs.

Therefore directly supplying one true effort covariate is not, by itself, a process-confounding correction. This result cannot be generalized to real GBIF effort because the covariate is simulation-privileged and the predictor universe differs from the primary condition.

## Scientific conclusion

The experiment separates two bottlenecks:

1. **Learner capacity / compatibility.** Pairing HGB with a nonlinear Random Forest increases authorization and positive-state recovery relative to HGB + standardized linear consensus.
2. **Observation-process confounding.** The unsafe observation-confounded thermal attribution persists despite nonlinear consensus and despite the privileged effort sensitivity.

So the next method problem is **not** simply to choose a more flexible second learner. A successor must represent the observation process in the estimand or measurement model, rather than treating learner agreement or one nuisance effort covariate as a safety certificate.
