## Over-resolution diagnosis

Five of 25 target-unresolved cells were sharpened to `replaceable`:

- shared-carrier seed 23002: thermal and water
- shared-carrier seed 23007: thermal and water
- null-correlated seed 23002: thermal

The four shared-carrier errors expose a logical gap in the v2 abstention helper. It only converted identical-closure **positive** states to unresolved. But if two processes are represented by exactly the same carrier closure, a sharp `replaceable` state is also process-specific over-resolution: the data challenged the same joint information removal for both process labels and cannot distinguish them.

Therefore the identical-closure abstention rule must apply to **all sharp states** (`replaceable|contributory|required`), not only positive states.

## Learner-capacity diagnosis

All 14 positive interaction cells were called replaceable. The occurrence learner in v2 is linear logistic regression with main effects only, while W5 is generated by a strong thermal × water interaction. A learner that cannot represent the full-world interaction cannot use process removal to identify its information.

This is not evidence that thermal and water are ecologically replaceable in W5. It is evidence that process identification is conditional on full-model representational adequacy, and the current absolute score floor alone does not guarantee mechanism-relevant learner capacity.

## Decision

**Development v2 is not ready for prospective freezing.**

Two distinct development issues remain:

1. **logical attribution boundary:** identical process closures must force unresolved for any sharp process state;
2. **learner capacity / positive recovery:** the linear occurrence learner fails to recover nearly all positive process targets, especially all W5 interactions.

The next development version may repair these issues only on already-burned development seeds. No KT threshold is frozen from v2 and no v2 result may be reused as prospective evidence.

The v2 result is nevertheless a major improvement over v1: the estimator/scoring prior mismatch is fixed, overall bounded-state behavior is mostly correct, and the remaining failures are now scientifically interpretable rather than dominated by artificial unavailability.
